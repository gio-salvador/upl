# Doctrine decisions

The author's decisions on questions the text left open, and the wording proposed to carry each
one into the teachings. For the author, and for anyone or any agent drafting a teaching
afterwards, so that a decision taken once is not asked again or contradicted.

This page is not a teaching. Where it and a page under `content/` disagree, the page under
`content/` is right and this page needs fixing. A decision here changes no page by itself: each
proposal below is applied only when the author approves that exact wording (locked decision 2),
in a pull request called out as a wording change.

## Decisions of 18 to 21 September 2026

Taken by the author, Giovanni S. Salvador, in answer to the three conflicts recorded in the
[cross-reference matrix](cross-reference.md) and the questions that followed from them.

### D1. God is impersonal: the source of light (closes X02 when applied)

In the author's words: "Impersonal, although god is the source of light, and we are light from
the source, this light is pure and vibrates multiple energies on the same spectrum as love.
Similar to seichonoie teachings."

The points settled with it:

1. **Source beyond.** God is the source; the universe and we are light flowing from it. The
   source is more than what it emits. UPL stands close to pantheism and is not the same as it.
2. **Love stays; will is reworded.** "God's love" is kept and explained once as the nature of
   the light itself, not the feeling of a person. "Divine will" is reworded towards "divine
   order", since a principle has no will.
3. **Prayer is attunement.** Prayer aligns the person with the light: affirmation, gratitude,
   stillness, listening inwardly. Where a borrowed prayer addresses God as a person, those words
   are read as poetry.
4. **Spiritual language, not physics.** Light, energy and spectrum are UPL's spiritual imagery.
   The text does not claim that physics proves them, which keeps to the existing rule that
   quantum physics is offered as an analogy.
5. **Darkness is the absence of light.** Darkness has no source of its own. It is where the
   light is obscured, by ignorance, fear or harmful action. Suffering and illness are real and
   are met with real help. This is where UPL parts from Seicho-No-Ie, which teaches that they
   are not ultimately real.
6. **"Child of God" is credited, not adopted.** It is named as Seicho-No-Ie's phrase when that
   tradition is described. UPL's own term stays "light from the source" or "emanation of divine
   light".

### D2. UPL heals the spirit; medicine treats the body (closes X01 when applied)

First decided as "medicine first; spiritual practice supports it", then refined by the author on
21 September 2026. The refined position:

1. UPL's own work is the healing of the spirit.
2. Illness of the body is met with science and medicine.
3. Medical science still has much to learn, and many conditions are not understood at all. It
   has proven effective wherever understanding has grown, and it will keep improving.
4. A follower of UPL never relies on alternative or spiritual remedies alone.

Two points in the author's statement are open, and P2 below is drafted on the cautious side of
both until the author settles them:

- **"Faith and belief can cure a person; Jesus cured solely with his words."** Read as a claim
  about physical disease, this contradicts Engaging with Holy Texts (scripture is read as
  teaching, not as literal record), D1 point 5 (illness is real; UPL parts from Seicho-No-Ie
  here) and point 4 above. P2 says instead that faith heals the spirit and strengthens a person
  in illness, and honours the healings of Jesus as teaching about the power of faith.
- **"Especially when AI merges with quantum computing."** A prediction, not a belief. It would
  date the page, and it adds a second speculative claim about quantum science to a text whose
  physics page is the one sceptical readers test. P2 keeps "it will keep improving" and leaves
  the forecast out.

Any teaching on healing taken from another tradition is adopted only as a complement to
medicine.

### D3. The disagreement with Buddhism is stated (closes X03 when applied)

UPL takes the path of mindfulness, ethical conduct and compassion, and karma, from Buddhism. It
does not take the teaching of non-self: with Hinduism, UPL holds that the soul is real and
immortal. The text says so openly, and no longer describes Buddhism as affirming suffering as
inherent to life, because the Buddha's teaching on suffering is a diagnosis with a cure.

## Proposed wording, waiting for the author

is under `content/1-foundations/` or `content/2-doctrine/`, so after it is applied the author

### For D2

**P1. Holistic Well-being, section introduction. Wording change. Not locked.**

- Before: "Central to its philosophy is the promotion of sobriety, a cautious engagement with
  science and modern medicine, and a clear stance against the use of recreational and illegal
  drugs."
- After: "Central to its philosophy is the promotion of sobriety, trust in science and modern
  medicine, and a clear stance against the use of recreational and illegal drugs."
- Why: "cautious" contradicts the title of the page beneath it and D2.

**P2. Trust in Science and Modern Medicine, "Balanced Approach to Health". Wording change. Not
locked.**

- Before: "Trust in science and evidence-based medicine is considered complementary to spiritual
  practices, offering solutions where traditional methods may fall short."
- After: "UPL's own work is the healing of the spirit. Faith, prayer and a mind at peace
  strengthen a person in illness, and the traditions honour this: the Gospels tell of Jesus
  healing with a word, and Judaism holds that saving a life comes before almost every other
  duty. UPL reads the accounts of healing as it reads all holy texts, as teaching about the
  power of faith, and not as a promise that faith will cure a disease. Illness of the body is
  met with science and medicine. Medical science still has much to learn, and many conditions
  are not yet understood, but it has proven effective wherever understanding has grown, and it
  will keep improving. A follower of UPL never relies on spiritual or alternative remedies
  alone: when a person is ill, medical care is sought without delay and followed, and spiritual
  practice walks beside it."
- Why: the old sentence reads as if medicine were the fallback. The first sentence of the
  paragraph is kept as it is. Revised on 21 September 2026 to carry the refined D2. Two voices,
  as the balance rule asks: one speaks for faith, one for medicine.
- To check: the Jewish principle is pikuach nefesh; confirm it against a source before it
  lands.

### For D3

**P3. Comparative Analysis, the paragraph on Hinduism and Buddhism. Wording change. Not
locked.**

- Before: "Similar to Hinduism and Buddhism, UPL posits karma and dharma as guiding principles
  of spiritual evolution, but it refrains from concepts like the caste system or affirming
  suffering as an inherent characteristic of life."
- After: "Similar to Hinduism and Buddhism, UPL posits karma and dharma as guiding principles of
  spiritual evolution. It does not take up the caste system. With Buddhism it shares the path of
  mindfulness, ethical conduct and compassion, and it honours the Buddha's teaching that
  suffering can be understood and brought to an end. It parts from Buddhism on the self:
  Buddhism teaches that there is no permanent self, while UPL holds, as Hinduism does, that the
  soul is real and immortal."
- Why: D3. It also removes the contradiction with the page on the Buddha.

### For D1

locked.**

- Before: "Compared to monotheistic religions like Christianity, Islam, and Judaism, UPL doesn't

  universe."
- Why: D1. "Doesn't focus on" left open whether such a deity exists; the new text says what UPL

**P10. Unity: no change proposed.**

The page speaks of "Tawhid, Islam's concept of the oneness and absolute sovereignty of God".
That sentence describes Islam's concept in Islam's terms, not UPL's God, and rewording
"sovereignty" would misdescribe Tawhid (balance rule 4: a tradition's own terms, used
correctly). The page does lean on Islam alone. That is the known imbalance, and it is better
answered when the ten core beliefs are given voices from several traditions than by a change
made for D1.

**The Author Note: no change proposed.** "God's infinite love" stays, under D1 point 2.

## What these decisions open

The decisions were the precondition for drawing more widely on the traditions. With them taken:

- Prayers that address God (the Lord's Prayer, Al-Fatiha, "lead me from darkness to light") can
  be given as examples, read under D1 point 3.
- Seicho-No-Ie's teaching on healing is adopted only as a complement to medicine (D2), and its
  teaching that sickness is not real is not adopted (D1 point 5).
- Further teachings from Buddhism can be taken up with the disagreement on the self already
  stated (D3).
